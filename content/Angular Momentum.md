Angular [[momentum]] is defined as $\vec{L}$ and is a vector quantity.

As $\sum \vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}, \to \sum \vec{\tau} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt}$

For a particle that is not rotating, we have a cross product equation, just like [[Torque|torque]]:
$$
\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}
$$
Where:
- $\vec{r}$ is the position vector from the axis of rotation to the center of mass of the moving object.
- $\vec{p}$ is the linear momentum of the object
The magnitude is of this is 
$$
 L = rp \sin\theta
$$
$$
L = r m v \sin \theta
$$
For a rigid object with shape, that is rotating, the linear momentum is:
$$
\vec{L} = I \vec{\omega}
$$
Note that this is different from [[Rotational Kinetic Energy]].

The conservation of angular momentum:
$$
\sum \vec{\tau} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = 0 \implies \sum \vec{L}_{i} =  \sum \vec{L}_{f}
$$
