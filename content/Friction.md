- The force of friction is always parallel to surfaces
- Opposes motion 
- Independent of $F_{a}$
	- The object might be moving to the right for example, and the force applied and the force of friction could be in the same direction
Friction is equal to:
$$
|\vec{F}_{f}| \leq \mu |\vec{F}_{N}|
$$
The two types of friction are:
- Static friction: when the two surfaces do not slide relative to each other
	- $F_{sf} \leq \mu_{s}F_{N}$
	- $F_{sf_{max}} \leq \mu_{s}F_{N}$
- Kinetic friction: when the two surfaces do slide relative to each other 
	- $F_{kf} = \mu_{k}F_{N}$

The coefficient of Friction is defined as $\mu$
- Experimentally determined
- no units
- value depends on the contacting surfaces
- range is between 0 - 2 typically
- $\mu_{s} > \mu_{k}$
